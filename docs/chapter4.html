Voltage Protection

Cell voltage is the primary health signal in a Li-ion pack. Every protection decision — from OVP to UVP — is derived from measuring voltage accurately at the per-cell level. This section explains why per-cell monitoring is non-negotiable, what physics occurs at the threshold boundaries, and how the BMS enforces them.

Why Per-Cell Voltage Monitoring is Non-Negotiable

Pack voltage hides weak cells. This is the single most important lesson in BMS design.

⚡ The Hidden Weak Cell Problem — 14S Pack Example

Cell 9 (red, 2.3V) is below the 2.5V UVP threshold and being damaged by copper dissolution. The 13 healthy cells at 3.5V produce a pack total of 47.8V — well within the expected 14S range of 45.5–49V. A pack-level BMS never sees the fault. Only per-cell AFE monitoring catches it.

⚡
Pack Voltage Measurement Cannot Replace Per-Cell Monitoring

A 14S LFP pack has a nominal voltage range of 35V (empty) to 51.1V (full). One cell at 2.3V (deeply discharged, damaged) while others are at 3.5V gives a pack voltage of 47.8V — a "normal" reading. The damaged cell continues to be discharged, dissolving copper current collector material into the electrolyte. Copper deposits then plate onto the separator, creating internal shorts. This failure chain is invisible without per-cell voltage monitoring.

Overvoltage Protection (OVP) — The 3.65V Hard Wall

LFP cells must not be charged above 3.65V per cell. Above this threshold, the cathode chemistry begins to destabilise in ways that cause permanent, cumulative damage.

LFP OVP Threshold Physics Normal charge ceiling: V_cell ≤ 3.65V (CC-CV target) OVP alarm: V_cell > 3.65V → stop charge, log event OVP hard cutoff: V_cell > 3.70V → open charge FET immediately OVP recovery hysteresis: do not allow charge resume until V_cell < 3.60V Hysteresis prevents rapid FET cycling (chattering) near the threshold. Without hysteresis, the FET would open at 3.65V, voltage sags to 3.64V, FET closes, voltage rises to 3.65V again — thousands of cycles per second, destroying the gate driver. The 50mV hysteresis window gives the charger time to ramp down gracefully.

What physically happens above 3.65V:

  • 3.65–3.80V: Electrolyte oxidation at cathode surface accelerates — CO₂ generation begins, internal pressure builds, SEI growth rate increases 5–10×
  • 3.80–4.00V: Lithium plating begins on cathode (Li deposits as metal, not intercalated) — irreversible capacity loss begins
  • Above 4.00V: Rapid gas generation, possible venting; cathode structural degradation accelerates sharply
📊 OVP Event — Voltage, Current, and FET State Timeline

Cell voltage rises during charge (blue). At 3.65V the BMS asserts OVP and opens the charge FET within <1s (firmware path) or <100ms (hardware comparator path). Current drops to zero. Voltage relaxes toward OCV (~3.55–3.60V) as the surface concentration gradient dissipates.

Undervoltage Protection (UVP) — The 2.5V Floor

The UVP threshold of 2.5V is chosen because below this point, the copper current collector on the anode begins to dissolve — an irreversible failure mechanism with no recovery path.

⚠
Copper Dissolution — The UVP Physical Basis

At voltages below ~2.0V, Cu²⁺ ions dissolve from the copper anode current collector into the electrolyte. These ions migrate toward the cathode and plate as metallic copper dendrites. These dendrites penetrate the separator, creating a permanent internal short circuit. A cell that has been deeply discharged to 0V then recharged will exhibit dramatically increased self-discharge (the internal short slowly drains it) and may fail catastrophically on the next charge cycle. The UVP threshold of 2.5V provides a 500mV safety margin above the copper dissolution onset.

UVP Thresholds and Recovery Procedure UVP warning: V_cell < 2.8V → reduce load, alert host system UVP hard cutoff: V_cell < 2.5V → open discharge FET immediately Deep discharge: V_cell < 2.0V → flag for inspection, pre-charge required Recovery hysteresis: charge resume only after V_cell > 2.8V (post pre-charge) If a cell reaches UVP, the BMS must not allow normal CC charge to resume immediately. The pre-charge sequence (see Section 4.4) first applies a low current (C/20) until all cells are above 2.8V, then transitions to normal CC-CV charge. This safely brings deeply discharged cells back without triggering lithium plating from high-rate charge on a partially failed SEI.

Voltage Measurement Accuracy — Why 16-bit AFE Matters

The OVP and UVP thresholds are tight windows. OVP fires at 3.65V; normal full charge is 3.65V — only a few millivolts separate "charged" from "fault". The AFE must measure with enough resolution to avoid both false positives and missed faults.

AFE Resolution Calculation Full voltage range per cell: 2.0V to 4.0V = 2.0V span 12-bit ADC: 2.0V / 4096 = 0.49 mV/LSB → ±1 mV accuracy (2 LSB) 16-bit ADC: 2.0V / 65536 = 0.031 mV/LSB → ±0.1 mV accuracy A 12-bit measurement gives ±1 mV. In the LFP plateau where dOCV/dSoC ≈ 0.2 mV/%, this ±1 mV translates to ±5% SoC uncertainty. A 16-bit ADC reduces this to ±0.5% SoC — critical for accurate balancing decisions. Production AFE ICs (TI BQ76952, ADI LTC6813) use 16-bit delta-sigma ADCs with integrated calibration references to achieve ±1.0 mV absolute accuracy across temperature.
